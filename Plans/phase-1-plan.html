Critical path and blockers

Blocker · highest priority

D8's scope is ambiguous, and the gap is two to three weeks of work

“The infrastructure required to support the Burstable MLaaS offering” admits two readings:

Reading A · narrow · ~1–1.5 weeks The supporting AWS foundation around the static servers — account and landing zone, VPC, IAM, storage, transfer path, access tier, logging — all under Terraform. Largely implied by D1–D7 anyway.
Reading B · broad · 3–4 weeks The elastic compute layer that makes the offering “Burstable” — on-demand GPU capacity beyond the fixed three servers, with cost guardrails. This is what Phase 2 previously held.

Because the updated Phase 2 contains no build work, Reading B is the more likely intent — and it is the difference between a comfortable Phase 1 and an over-committed one.

Recommended position, pending confirmation: commit to Reading A as the baseline, plus a time-boxed Reading B foundation — a burst-capable design decision record and one working burst proof point on the same landing zone. Explicitly exclude the app tier, multi-tenancy, platform services, and CI/CD; those remain Work Scope Amendment territory.

Action: confirm D8 scope with Michael Russo at the GE kickoff on 6 October.

Helpful development: GE Vernova has CloudFormation templates and YAML available, including Karpenter scaling configuration — exactly the mechanism WS7 proposes for the burst layer. A working reference config materially de-risks Reading B. Get these files at or before the kickoff, then reassess the 3–4 week estimate.

Blocker · new

The application / infrastructure / support boundary has shifted

Earlier analysis placed the Java Spring and React application tier outside LTI's scope, on the strength of the August role split: Jim Tallman's team owns the application, LTI owns infrastructure.

The internal kickoff brief says something different — LTI will "work with Jim Tallman and his engineering team to deploy their application and frontend into the new environment, then support it."

Deploying and supporting an application someone else builds is not the same as building it, but it is more than a pure-infrastructure boundary, and it carries real work: container images, manifests, CI/CD into the cluster, runtime troubleshooting, and an on-call expectation running through all three months of Phase 2.

Action: the kickoff brief itself says to confirm the division of application, infrastructure and support responsibilities at kickoff. Do that on 6 October and write the answer down. No application deployment materials have been provided yet — only infrastructure templates.

Blocker · highest priority

The hardware spec and the funding are both unsettled

The Electrification business case Mike Russo shared at the kickoff states the funded footprint as 4 × p5.48xlarge — 32 H100 GPUs. SOW deliverable D1 says 3 static servers with 24 total GPUs.

This is not a rounding difference. It changes the service-quota request, the capacity reservation, the cost basis, the landing-zone sizing, and how the D7 multi-GPU demo is designed. Nothing should be requested from AWS until it is settled.

Compounding it: the deck lists “Secure funding from business for GPU Servers” as an open next step. The money is Electrification 2026 program money and it is not yet committed — which makes funding a predecessor to the phase’s first milestone.

Action: confirm the server count and the funding status with Mike Russo this week. These two together now outrank the D8 scope question.

Instance selection: 8 GPUs per server

Effectively decided: p5.48xlarge (8 × H100 80GB) — named in the Electrification plan, with AWS capacity discussions for P5 already underway.

But the criterion that matters for D7 is Alpharetta parity, not raw performance. D7 asks for equivalent training, and H100 is several generations newer than typical on-prem hardware — a generation mismatch changes numerics, driver and framework requirements, and achievable batch sizes. Confirm Alpharetta’s GPU model and decide explicitly how “equivalent” is judged against newer silicon.

Cost — now quantified

The Electrification plan prices 4 × p5.48xlarge with a 24% GE Vernova customer discount:

OptionStructureTotal
1On-demand for 15 months, billed quarterly$1,832,171.52
212-month all-up-front, then 3 months on-demand$1,228,287.79
Option 2 saving$603,883.73

That is ~$122,145 per month on-demand for all four servers ($30,536.19 per server), or roughly $71,821 per month equivalent if prepaid for twelve.

Two consequences. This is an order of magnitude above the “tens of thousands per month” this plan previously assumed — the earlier figure was for three servers of unspecified family, against four of the most expensive one. And a 12-month prepay removes the stop/start lever: if the business takes Option 2, idle-shutdown saves nothing on committed servers and the cost argument for building it disappears. Find out which option was chosen before designing a scheduling policy.

The 15-month costing horizon is itself a signal — the SOW runs five months, to February 2027, but the hardware is costed to roughly January 2028.
